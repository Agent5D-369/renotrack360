# Retained milestone billing

New proposal reviews may retain a structured billing schedule alongside the existing payment-terms text. The editable starting schedule is 20% execution/mobilization, 30% after reviewed rough-in inspection records, 40% at reviewed substantial completion, and 10% after reviewed final walkthrough, punch, and closeout conditions. Percentages must be positive, use at most two decimal places, and total exactly 100%. The documented deposit must equal the first computed draw. Each non-final draw rounds half-up to cents; the final draw receives the exact residual. A schedule that would produce a zero or negative draw is rejected.

The structured schedule is part of the immutable EstimateSnapshot accepted by the client. Older snapshots remain readable, but absence of a structured schedule is shown as missing agreement coverage and cannot be replaced with defaults after acceptance. Service-template milestones remain planning inputs; they do not authorize billing.

After the accepted estimate is converted to its job and the owner adopts a matching JobFinancialBaseline, the owner can review a retained milestone and create one DRAFT invoice. The review digest binds the accepted snapshot, baseline, amounts, and existing milestone drafts. A company finance lock, request digest, and unique snapshot/milestone constraint make retries safe. The invoice, immutable MilestoneInvoiceDraft link, and audit event commit atomically. No trigger is inferred from job status, and creation does not send an invoice, create a payment link, record a payment, or promote the invoice from DRAFT.

Existing non-void invoices without a retained milestone link stop milestone drafting. This prevents double billing until a later explicit allocation workflow can review historical invoices and receipts. Linked invoices retain their reviewed number, job, client, subtotal, tax, and total; ordinary note and due-date edits remain available. The retained link prevents invoice deletion independently at the database boundary.

Verification uses `npm run test:milestone-billing` against a disposable loopback database, plus migration replay, typecheck, and build. Tests cover schedule bounds and cent residuals, deposit agreement, current owner/company gates, accepted/conversion/baseline prerequisites, stale review refusal, concurrent retry, once-only drafting, legacy and unallocated invoice refusal, immutable history, linked invoice guards, and audit rollback. Fixtures never send invoices or create provider transactions.
